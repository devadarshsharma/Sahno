# Sahno Commercial Strategy

**Status:** Working commercial strategy  
**Last updated:** 27 August 2026
**Related source of truth:** `../Sahno_Product_Discovery_and_MVP.md`

## 1. Commercial intent

Sahno is intended to become a sustainable side business, not only a portfolio project. Commercial decisions should reinforce the product's core purpose: helping groups move opportunities from enquiry through confirmation, preparation, and completion without important work being lost.

The working commercial principle is:

> Members participate for free. Organisations pay to operate more effectively.

This is directional rather than a final pricing commitment.

## 2. Who receives the value

### Members

Members need a low-friction way to:

- respond to availability requests;
- distinguish tentative from confirmed commitments;
- see where and when they need to attend;
- understand their responsibilities;
- receive important changes and reminders; and
- access event discussion and resources.

These participation features should generally remain free. Charging individual members would create adoption friction for the organisation and does not align with where most of the measurable value is created.

### Organisations and organisers

Organisers receive the strongest operational and financial value. Sahno can reduce manual chasing, missed information, preventable booking mistakes, and forgotten payment obligations. It can also give organisers a clearer view of enquiries, upcoming work, and readiness.

The organisation should therefore be the paying customer, with subscription and billing owned at the organisation level rather than by individual members.

## 3. Working packaging model

The initial packaging hypothesis is a simple freemium model with one paid tier. It should remain easy to explain and test.

| Capability | Free | Sahno Pro |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Member participation | Yes | Yes |
| Availability and RSVP | Yes | Yes |
| Member schedule/calendar | Yes | Yes |
| Event details, discussion, and responsibilities | Core access | Full access |
| Members per organisation | Limited; exact limit open | Higher or unlimited |
| Active/upcoming engagements | Limited; exact limit open | Higher or unlimited |
| Files and storage | Limited | Increased |
| Non-responder reminders | Manual/basic | Automated and configurable |
| Organiser dashboard | Basic | Full attention/readiness view |
| Enquiry pipeline | Not included or limited | Included |
| Customer history | Not included or limited | Included |
| Event readiness | Basic checklist | Advanced rules and views |
| Payment tracking | Not included | Included |
| Reports and financial summaries | Not included | Included |
| Multiple organisers and permissions | Basic | Advanced |
| Travel management | Not included | Potential future capability |

The free tier must be useful enough for a real group to adopt and complete the core workflow. Limits should create a natural upgrade point without making member participation frustrating.

## 4. Pricing hypothesis

The current discussion suggests testing **A$15–25 per organisation per month** for Sahno Pro.

This is a hypothesis, not a locked price. Before committing, validate:

- willingness to pay with real organisers;
- which feature or limit causes an upgrade;
- whether annual billing is attractive;
- whether small volunteer groups require a lower entry point;
- whether professional groups support a higher tier later; and
- the cost of notifications, file storage, support, and payment processing.

Avoid introducing several paid tiers until usage shows genuinely different customer segments. A possible future higher tier should be earned by evidence, not created for pricing-page appearance.

## 5. Paid-value priorities

Paid capabilities should save organiser time, reduce operational risk, or clarify money. The strongest candidates are:

1. The full organiser attention dashboard.
2. Automated reminders and escalation rules.
3. Enquiry and booking pipeline management.
4. Advanced event-readiness checks.
5. Customer history and repeat-booking context.
6. Performer payment and outstanding-balance tracking.
7. Reports and commercial summaries.
8. Advanced roles and permissions.
9. Increased storage and future integrations.

Do not weaken the member experience to manufacture a paywall. Members must be able to participate in an organisation's workflow without purchasing their own plan.

## 6. Go-to-market approach

### Customer Zero

Use Australian Qawwal Party (AQP) as the initial real-world pilot. Record every genuine enquiry and engagement in Sahno while existing tools remain available during validation.

### Initial customer profile

Start with small community and performing groups where one or more organisers currently coordinate bookings through messaging, personal calendars, spreadsheets, and memory. Initial segments include:

- bands and music groups;
- choirs;
- dance and theatre groups;
- cultural associations;
- spiritual or community organisations; and
- volunteer event groups.

### Early acquisition

Begin with founder-led outreach and referrals through adjacent groups. Demonstrate the full enquiry-to-completion workflow rather than advertising isolated features. The clearest promise is:

> Sahno tells organisers what needs attention and tells members what they need to know.

## 7. Validation plan

Commercial validation should run alongside product validation.

1. Use Sahno for approximately 10–20 real engagements with Customer Zero.
2. Measure time spent chasing responses and missing details before and after use.
3. Interview several organisers outside AQP using the workflow, not only a feature list.
4. Show the proposed Free/Pro split and ask which capabilities they would pay for.
5. Test A$15, A$20, and A$25 monthly price points without implying that any is final.
6. Seek early paid commitments or deposits once the organiser workflow reliably works.
7. Test a second organisation type before generalising terminology or packaging.

Useful evidence includes:

- organisations completing the core workflow repeatedly;
- fewer manual follow-ups;
- fewer missed or late critical details;
- organisers voluntarily inviting their whole group;
- regular use of the attention dashboard; and
- real willingness to pay, not only positive feedback.

## 8. Commercial guardrails

- Do not turn Sahno into accounting software.
- Do not charge every member merely to access an organisation's events.
- Do not place essential day-of information behind a member paywall.
- Do not add complex pricing before the upgrade trigger is understood.
- Do not prioritise broad-market features over the validated Customer Zero workflow.
- Do not treat a proposed tier, limit, or price as locked until it is recorded in the decision log.
- Protect financial, customer, and private member information through explicit permissions.

## 9. Subscription cancellation, downgrade, and data retention

Cancelling Sahno Pro does not delete the organisation or its material.

1. The Owner requests cancellation.
2. Paid capabilities remain available until the current paid billing period ends.
3. The organisation then moves to Free or a clearly labelled Restricted state.
4. Existing organisation data is preserved. If the organisation exceeds Free-tier limits, existing over-limit material remains readable and exportable, but additional paid-only or over-limit creation may be blocked.
5. Reactivation restores paid functionality without requiring the organisation to be rebuilt.

Payment failure follows a grace and recovery path before downgrade. It does not immediately remove access or delete data.

Permanent deletion is an independent Owner-only workflow. It must use re-authentication, explicit confirmation, notice to other Admins, an export opportunity, and a recovery window. Exact grace and retention periods are not yet locked.

This policy is intended to make subscription cancellation safe and predictable while preserving a legitimate upgrade incentive through feature and capacity limits rather than fear of data loss.

## 10. Open commercial decisions

- Exact Free and Pro limits.
- Final launch price and billing currency strategy.
- Monthly versus annual billing and any annual discount.
- Trial length, if any.
- Exact failed-payment grace period and post-cancellation retention period.
- Export formats and permanent organisation-deletion recovery window.
- Whether payment tracking is entirely Pro or has a limited free form.
- Whether professional organisations eventually need a second paid tier.
- Founder-led support expectations and service boundaries.
- Tax, business-registration, trademark, domain, and payment-provider decisions.
- Success thresholds for moving from private pilot to paid beta and public launch.
